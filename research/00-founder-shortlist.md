# Founder's own shortlist (written before the research came back)

Written so the agents' pitches can be compared against an unanchored first take.

The hinge gives us four things no other phone has:
1. a continuous analog value (0–180°), readable at ~5°;
2. the speed of the fold;
3. two physical planes at a real angle to each other (a lid, a ramp, a bucket, a page);
4. a physical "shut" that hides the inner screen.

Squish already owns #4 plus "close slowly onto an object". So the new game should live on #1 and #3,
in the 60–180° range where the inner screen is lit, with slow-to-medium motions (kind to the hinge and to
the ~5° accuracy).

| # | Working name | Hinge mapping | Loop | Gut score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Pour** | The raised half is a bucket of sand. Tip it (fold from flat toward 90°) and the sand pours across the crease, faster the steeper. Sand only starts moving past its angle of repose (~30° of tilt), like real sand. | Jars slide past on a conveyor on the lower half. Fill each one to its line. Perfect fills refill your hopper; spills waste it. The run ends when the hopper runs dry. | 8.5 |
| B | Skee-fold | Upper half is a ramp: its steepness sets the ball's speed. | Roll balls into scoring rings on the lower half. | 6 |
| C | Bellows | Each fold stroke pumps air. | Inflate balloons as big as you dare before they pop (push your luck). | 6.5 (wear worry: constant pumping) |
| D | Throttle flyer | Angle = altitude of a glider. | Endless cave. | 6 (works on any phone with a slider) |

A is the front-runner: the motion (tipping a container) is the most natural thing a hinged object can do, the
"angle of repose" makes the control feel physically true, falling sand is one of the most watchable things you
can put on a screen, and the loop (precision + push-your-luck + a resource that refills on perfection) is the
shape of Stack and Threes, not of a toy.
